The note

Negation is two pieces that sandwich the conjugated verb. Here the verb is « avait », not « entendu » — so « ne » and « pas » wrap around « avait » and leave the past participle outside. The object pronoun « l’ » squeezes in between « ne » and the verb.

The rule
Negation wraps the conjugated verb
French negates with a pair: « ne » before the verb, « pas » after it. In compound tenses the pair closes around the helping verb, not the participle. Je n’ai pas mangé, never Je n’ai mangé pas.

Layer 1 — Newsreader

Le vieil homme

The French. A text serif with optical sizing, so it stays warm at 35px instead of turning brittle. It carries the accents without fuss.

Layer 2 — IBM Plex Mono

the / old / man

The gloss. Monospace reads as apparatus, not prose. Nobody mistakes it for something to read straight through.

Layer 3 — Archivo

The old man closed the door.

The English and every control. A plain grotesque that stays legible at 12px and never argues with the serif.
