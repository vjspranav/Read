---
id: de-after-negation
name: After a negation, «un» and «du» collapse to «de»
category: negation
---

A negated verb strips the article down to a bare «de» (or «d'»). The thing
doesn't exist, so French stops marking how much of it there is.

- Il y a **un** bureau. → Il n'y a plus **de** bureau.
- Elle pose **des** questions. → Elle ne pose pas **de** questions.
- Je bois **du** café. → Je ne bois pas **de** café.

The definite article survives untouched: «je n'aime pas **le** café» — I don't
like coffee in general. And «être» is exempt: «ce n'est pas **un** train».
