---
id: partitive-article
name: «du», «de la», «des» — some, without saying how much
category: articles
---

When you're talking about part of something rather than a whole, specific
object, French doesn't leave the noun bare — it puts a small word in front
meaning roughly "some", even in places English drops it entirely.

- Elle achète **du** pain. — She buys (some) bread. «pain» is masculine, so
  «de» plus «le» contracts to «du».
- Elle achète **de la** confiture. — Some jam. «confiture» is feminine, so
  «de la» stays whole.
- Elle achète **des** croissants. — Some croissants. Plural always takes
  «des», whatever the gender.

English can say "bread" and mean some bread. French can't leave the noun on
its own like that — one of «du», «de la», «des» has to be there.
