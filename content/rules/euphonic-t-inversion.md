---
id: euphonic-t-inversion
name: The extra «-t-» that stops two vowels colliding
category: verbs
---

To ask a question, French can flip the verb and its subject: «il a» becomes
«a-t-il». «a» and «il» are both vowel sounds, and saying them back to back is
awkward, so a «t» gets inserted between them purely for the sound. It doesn't
mean anything on its own — it's only there to make the question sayable.

- Il **a** un parapluie. → **A-t-il** un parapluie ? — never «a-il».
- Elle **arrive**. → **Arrive-t-elle** demain ?
- Il **dit** vrai. → **Dit-il** vrai ? — «dit» already ends on a consonant
  sound, so no extra «-t-» is needed.

You only see this «-t-» after a verb form that ends in a vowel, like «-e» or
«-a». Once the verb already ends in a consonant, like «dit» or «part», the
subject just attaches directly.
