---
id: etre-a-possession
name: «être à» — whose something is
category: prepositions
---

French often shows who owns something with «être» plus «à» plus the owner,
instead of a separate verb meaning "to belong". Word for word it's "is to
[someone]" — that's just how French says something belongs to them.

- Ce chat **est à** moi. — This cat is mine.
- **À qui est** ce sac ? — Whose bag is this? Literally "to whom is this bag".
- Le vélo **n'est à** personne. — The bike doesn't belong to anyone.

The owner can be a stressed pronoun (moi, lui, elle) or a full noun after
«à» — the shape of the phrase doesn't change either way.
