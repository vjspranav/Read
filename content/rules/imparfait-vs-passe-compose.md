---
id: imparfait-vs-passe-compose
name: Imparfait or passé composé — background versus event
category: verbs
---

Both describe the past, and in speech that's the choice most people actually
have to make. The imparfait (**-ait**, **-aient**) paints the scene: what was
going on, what was already true. The passé composé (**avoir**/**être** plus a
participle) is the thing that happened — one event, with a start and a finish.

- Il **pleuvait** depuis le matin. Léa **a couru** jusqu'à l'arrêt. — The rain
  is the backdrop; the running is the thing that happened.
- Le bus **n'était pas** encore là. — Still the backdrop: a state, not an
  event.
- Elle **a cherché** son parapluie, mais il **n'était pas** là. — One search
  (event) inside one unchanging fact (state).

Ask yourself: is this the picture, or is this the next thing that happened?
The picture takes imparfait; the next thing takes passé composé.
