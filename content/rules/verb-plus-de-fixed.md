---
id: verb-plus-de-fixed
name: Some verbs lock in «de» with no article after it
category: prepositions
---

A handful of French verbs always pair with «de» as part of the verb itself —
not as the word "of" and not as the "some" of the partitive. Nothing comes
between the verb and «de», and no article — «un», «le», «du» — ever follows
it.

- **changer de** rue — to change streets, to move to a different street.
- **avoir besoin de** temps — to need time.
- **se souvenir de** lui — to remember him.

English usually drops the preposition entirely in translation. Treat «verbe
+ de» as one glued unit and don't try to translate the «de» on its own.
