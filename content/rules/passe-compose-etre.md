---
id: passe-compose-etre
name: A handful of verbs build their past with «être», not «avoir»
category: verbs
---

Most verbs use «avoir» to build the passé composé. A small set of verbs —
mostly about movement or a change of state, like «aller», «arriver»,
«partir», «rester», «venir» — use «être» instead. When «être» is the helper,
the past participle agrees with the subject, the way an adjective does.

- Ils **sont restés** sous le parapluie. — «restés» because the subject,
  «ils», is plural.
- Elle **est arrivée** à l'heure. — «arrivée» gets an «e» because «elle» is
  feminine.
- Il **a couru**. — «courir» isn't in this set, so it keeps «avoir» and the
  participle never changes shape.

Reflexive verbs — the ones with «se» — always use «être» too, and agree the
same way.
